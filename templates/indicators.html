Indicator Guide.

Quick reference for all chart indicators. Parameters shown in parentheses. EMA Ribbon, Ichimoku, Bollinger Bands, RSI divergence, MACD, SuperTrend, Volume Profile, Smart Money Concepts, CPR, and more — all built into one platform.

Oscillators
RSI RSI(14)
Relative Strength Index — momentum oscillator (0–100)
Measures the speed and magnitude of recent price changes. Calculated as 100 - 100/(1 + avgGain/avgLoss) over 14 bars using Wilder's smoothing.
Look for: Overbought >70, oversold <30. Centerline (50) acts as trend filter. Divergences between RSI and price signal potential reversals.
RSI Divergence RSI(14), lookback=3
Detects bullish/bearish divergences between price swings and RSI swings
Finds swing highs/lows in price (checking 3 bars each side), reads RSI at those points, and compares. Regular divergence: price makes new high/low but RSI doesn't. Hidden divergence: RSI makes new extreme but price doesn't.
Look for: Green lines = bullish divergence (potential reversal up). Red lines = bearish divergence. Solid = regular, dashed = hidden. More reliable on HTF (1D/1W).
MACD MACD(12,26,9)
Moving Average Convergence Divergence — trend & momentum
MACD line = EMA(12) - EMA(26). Signal line = EMA(9) of MACD. Histogram = difference between MACD and signal.
Look for: MACD crossing above signal = bullish. Histogram expanding = momentum increasing. Zero-line crossover = trend change. Divergences with price are powerful reversal signals.
TTM Squeeze BB(30,2.0) inside KC(30,2.5), min 3 bars
Yellow segments when Bollinger Bands contract inside Keltner Channels
When BB upper < KC upper AND BB lower > KC lower for at least 3 consecutive bars, a "squeeze" is active — volatility is compressing. Originally by John Carter.
Look for: Squeeze ending = imminent explosive move. Direction determined by momentum (not shown). Combine with other indicators for direction bias. Longer squeezes → bigger breakouts.
BB Width % BB(20), lookback=120, threshold=20
Cyan segments when Bollinger Band width is in the bottom 20th percentile of its 120-bar history
Calculates BB width as (upper - lower) / basis, then ranks the current value as a percentile of the last 120 bars. When the percentile drops below 20, it signals extreme compression. Each bar is colored individually — brighter = more extreme compression, dimmer = near threshold — creating a heatmap effect that highlights hotspots within long squeeze stretches.
Look for: Cyan segments = unusually tight bands relative to recent history. Bright hotspots within a stretch mark the most extreme compression points. Often precedes a volatility expansion. More adaptive than TTM Squeeze — adjusts to the asset's own volatility regime.
ATR % ATR(14), lookback=100, threshold=20
Magenta segments when ATR is in the bottom 20th percentile of its 100-bar history
Calculates ATR(14) then ranks it as a percentile of the last 100 bars. When ATR drops into the bottom 20%, the asset is experiencing unusually low volatility. Each bar is colored individually — brighter = more extreme compression, dimmer = near threshold — creating a heatmap effect that highlights hotspots.
Look for: Magenta segments = abnormally low volatility. Bright hotspots mark the most compressed bars. Useful for identifying coiled-spring setups. Works on any asset type, not just those with Bollinger Bands. Combine with BBW% for confluence.
NR7 lookback=7
Amber segments on bars where the high-low range is the narrowest of the last 7 bars
Compares each bar's range (high - low) against the previous 6 bars. If it's the smallest, the bar is flagged as NR7.
Look for: NR7 bars = volatility contraction at the micro level. Historically followed by range expansion within 1-3 bars. Multiple NR7 bars in sequence = very strong compression. Classic Larry Williams / Toby Crabel concept.
Williams %R %R(14)
Orange oscillator, 0 to -100 scale
%R = ((Highest High - Close) / (Highest High - Lowest Low)) × -100 over 14 periods. Inverted scale: -20 = overbought, -80 = oversold. Mathematically the inverse of Fast Stochastic %K. Developed by Larry Williams.
Look for: Above -20 = overbought (potential reversal down). Below -80 = oversold (potential reversal up). In strong trends, %R can stay overbought/oversold for extended periods — this is a feature, not a bug. Best used as a timing tool within an established trend.
CCI CCI(20)
Cyan oscillator around zero, ±100 reference lines
CCI = (Typical Price - SMA of TP) / (0.015 × Mean Deviation). Typical Price = (H+L+C)/3. Measures how far price deviates from its statistical mean. Unbounded — can go well beyond ±100 in strong trends. Developed by Donald Lambert.
Look for: Above +100 = strong upward momentum (a trend-continuation reading, not necessarily overbought). Below -100 = strong downward momentum. Zero-line cross = momentum shift. CCI returning from extreme to zero is read as a mean-reversion condition. Works well for identifying cyclical turns.
Stochastic Stoch(14, 3, 3)
Blue %K + Orange %D, 0-100 scale
%K = smoothed ratio of (Close - Lowest Low) / (Highest High - Lowest Low) over 14 periods. %D = SMA(3) of %K. Measures where price closed relative to its recent range. 80/20 OB/OS zones.
Look for: %K crossing above %D below 20 = bullish signal. %K crossing below %D above 80 = bearish signal. Divergence between price highs/lows and stochastic = potential reversal. In strong trends, stochastic stays pinned to extremes — not a reversal signal.
Stoch RSI StochRSI(14, 14, 3, 3)
Purple %K + Pink %D, 0-100 scale
Applies the Stochastic formula to RSI values instead of price. More sensitive than standard Stochastic — reaches extremes more often. RSI(14) is calculated first, then Stochastic(14) is applied to the RSI series, smoothed with SMA(3) for %K and %D.
Look for: Very popular in crypto trading. Reaches OB/OS faster than Stochastic. %K/%D crosses at extremes are watched closely as momentum-exhaustion conditions. Combined with a trend filter, OS bounces have historically held up better in uptrends, and OB rejections in downtrends.
Trend & Moving Averages
MA SMA(20)
Simple Moving Average — 20-period average of closing prices
Equal-weighted average of the last 20 closes. Smooths out short-term noise. Commonly used as a short-term trend filter.
Look for: Price above MA = bullish bias. MA slope indicates trend direction. Price rejecting/bouncing off MA = support/resistance.
EMA EMA(21)
Exponential Moving Average — gives more weight to recent prices
Reacts faster than SMA to price changes due to exponential weighting. 21-period is standard for short-term trend following.
Look for: Same as MA but more responsive. EMA crossovers with price or other EMAs generate trend signals.
Daily EMAs EMA(21), EMA(50), EMA(200)
Cyan 21 Magenta 50 Yellow 200
Three key daily EMAs plotted on any timeframe. The 200 EMA is the most widely watched trend indicator. 21/50 are intermediate momentum filters.
Look for: "Golden cross" (50 above 200) = bull market. Price above all 3 = strong uptrend. 200 EMA often acts as dynamic support/resistance on pullbacks.
Weekly MAs SMA(50w), SMA(100w), SMA(200w)
Green 50 Orange 100 Red 200
Weekly SMAs computed server-side from weekly candles, interpolated to display on any timeframe. These are long-term trend anchors.
Look for: The 200W SMA is a widely watched long-term reference. Order of MAs (50>100>200) = bullish alignment. Weekly MA tests are levels many traders watch for a reaction.
Bull Market Support Band 21W EMA + 20W SMA
Teal EMA Red SMA
The area between the 21-week EMA and 20-week SMA. In bull markets, price finds support here on pullbacks.
Look for: Price bouncing off the band = bull continuation. Price closing below = potential bear phase. The band converging = major move incoming.
CM Ultimate MA VWMA(20)
Green when rising Red when falling
Chris Moody's color-coded moving average. A volume-weighted MA(20) — weighting each close by that bar's volume — that changes color based on direction: green when the MA is rising, red when falling.
Look for: Color change = early trend shift signal. Green-to-red flip = bearish. Red-to-green = bullish. Works best on daily and weekly timeframes.
EMA Ribbon EMA(30)/EMA(60), ATR(60) margin
Green bull Orange bear Yellow neutral
Two EMAs with color fill between them. Color determined by separation relative to ATR: green when fast EMA is sufficiently above slow (bullish trend), orange when below (bearish), yellow when within ATR margin (neutral/choppy).
Look for: Color transitions signal trend changes. Green→Yellow = weakening bull. Yellow→Orange = bear confirmed. Narrow fill = low conviction.
Consensio SMA(3, 7, 30)
Multi-MA alignment method of D. Tyler Jenks (Lucid Investment Strategies), implemented in Carrac — three SMAs whose ordering defines trend strength
Plots a short (3), medium (7), and long (30) period SMA on the chart. The ordering of the three lines against each other determines whether the timeframe reads full bullish, full bearish, or somewhere in between.
Look for: Short > Medium > Long = full consensio (strongest bullish alignment). Long > Medium > Short = full bearish. Partial orderings mark early-stage or weakening trends.
Consensio Lite fewer MAs, same alignment logic
Simplified Consensio overlay with less chart clutter
Same MA-alignment concept as Consensio, plotted with fewer moving averages for a cleaner chart. Trades some resolution for readability — a good fit for lower timeframes or when stacking with other overlays.
Look for: Same read as Consensio — MA ordering signals trend strength — with a lighter visual footprint.
Alligator SMMA(13,8,5) with offsets (8,5,3)
Bill Williams' Alligator — jaw, teeth, lips
Blue jaw SMMA(13) offset 8 bars. Red teeth SMMA(8) offset 5. Green lips SMMA(5) offset 3. "Sleeping" when intertwined, "eating" when spread apart.
Look for: Lines spreading apart = trending market. Intertwined = choppy/ranging. Lips crossing teeth crossing jaw = trend direction. Avoid trading when alligator is "sleeping".
Gaussian Channel Poles(4), Period(144), TR×1.414
Ehlers Gaussian Filter with filtered true range channel
Multi-pole Gaussian filter (EMA applied N times) creates an ultra-smooth trend line. Filtered true range × multiplier forms the channel. Green when filter rising, red when falling. Reduced Lag mode adds momentum, Fast Response averages with 1-pole for quicker reaction.
Look for: Price above channel = strong uptrend. Price inside channel = consolidation/transition. Color change = trend reversal. Channel width shows volatility — narrow = low vol squeeze, wide = expansion. Works best on higher timeframes (4H, 1D).
Ichimoku Cloud Tenkan(9), Kijun(26), Senkou B(52), Disp(26)
Complete trend system — cloud, conversion/base lines, lagging span
Tenkan-sen (9-period midpoint) and Kijun-sen (26-period midpoint) show momentum and support/resistance. The cloud (Kumo) is formed by Senkou Span A and B displaced 26 bars forward — green when bullish, red when bearish. Chikou Span is the close displaced 26 bars backward.
Look for: Price above cloud = bullish, below = bearish, inside = no trend. Tenkan crossing Kijun above the cloud is a strong bullish alignment condition. Thick cloud = strong support/resistance. Cloud twist (color change) = potential trend reversal. Chikou above price = bullish confirmation.
Pi Cycle Top SMA(111) vs SMA(350)×2
BTC cycle top indicator — cross signals macro tops
When the 111-day SMA crosses above 2× the 350-day SMA, it has historically coincided with Bitcoin cycle tops within 3 days.
Look for: Crossover = potential cycle top. Only relevant for BTC on daily timeframe. Convergence = approaching danger zone. Has called every BTC cycle top so far.
SMA 20/200 SMA(20) + SMA(200)
Blue SMA(20) + Gold SMA(200) plotted together
Plots two Simple Moving Averages on the same overlay — a fast SMA(20) and a slow SMA(200) — for tracking short- vs long-term trend context side by side.
Look for: Price above/below each line shows short- vs long-term trend bias. SMA(20) crossing SMA(200) marks a classic golden/death cross transition. Combine with other trend tools before acting.
Bollinger Bands BB(20, 2.0)
Volatility envelope around SMA(20)
Upper band = SMA(20) + 2×StdDev. Lower band = SMA(20) - 2×StdDev. Bands widen with volatility, contract during compression. Offset: shift bands ±N bars on the time axis; positive = lag the signal, negative = lead it.
Look for: Price touching upper band ≠ sell signal (can "walk the band" in trends). Squeeze (narrow bands) precedes breakout. W-bottom / M-top patterns at bands are reversal signals.
Keltner Tight EMA(35) ± ATR(14)×0.5
Tight Keltner Channel preset — tighter ATR-based envelope for mean-reversion
Center line is EMA(35). Upper/lower channels are ±0.5× ATR(14) from center. Tighter than the standard Keltner Channel (EMA 20, ATR 10, mult 1.5). Both visible simultaneously for wide + tight bands.
Look for: Price breaking outside channel = momentum shift. Riding the upper/lower channel = strong trend. Channel narrowing = compression.
Donchian Channels DC(20)
Green upper + Red lower + Gray midline
Upper channel = highest high over 20 periods. Lower channel = lowest low over 20 periods. Middle line = average of upper and lower. Originally used by Richard Donchian, popularized by the Turtle Traders.
Look for: Breakout above upper channel = potential new uptrend. Breakdown below lower channel = potential new downtrend. Channel width reflects volatility. Flat channels = consolidation. Classic trend-following systems built on this channel treat a new 20-period high as bullish continuation and a new 20-period low as bearish continuation.
Parabolic SAR AF: 0.02-0.20
Green dots below price (uptrend) + Red dots above price (downtrend)
SAR (Stop and Reverse) uses an acceleration factor that increases as the trend continues. Dots below price = bullish, dots above = bearish. When price crosses SAR, the indicator flips direction. Developed by J. Welles Wilder Jr.
Look for: SAR flip = potential trend reversal. Dots tightening toward price = trend losing momentum. Works best in trending markets. In choppy/sideways markets, generates frequent whipsaws — combine with a trend filter (e.g., ADX or moving averages).
Keltner Channels EMA(20) ± ATR(10)×1.5
Blue EMA basis + Orange upper/lower bands
Center line is EMA(20). Upper/lower channels are ±1.5× ATR(10) from center. Similar to Bollinger Bands but uses ATR instead of standard deviation, making it smoother and less reactive to sudden spikes.
Look for: Used with Bollinger Bands for TTM Squeeze detection — when BB contracts inside KC, volatility is compressed. Breakout above/below KC = strong momentum. Price riding upper KC = strong uptrend.
Hull MA HMA(20)
Magenta reduced-lag moving average
HMA = WMA(sqrt(n)) of [2×WMA(n/2) - WMA(n)]. Uses weighted moving averages to virtually eliminate lag while maintaining smoothness. Developed by Alan Hull.
Look for: HMA slope change = early trend reversal signal. Faster than EMA at detecting turns. Price above rising HMA = bullish, below falling HMA = bearish. Best used as a trend filter or trigger, not for support/resistance.
EMA Cross EMA(9) × EMA(21)
Cyan fast EMA + Orange slow EMA + cross markers
Plots EMA(9) and EMA(21) with triangle markers at crossover points. Green triangle below price = bullish cross (fast crosses above slow). Red diamond above price = bearish cross.
Look for: A bullish cross within an uptrend reads as trend continuation. Bearish cross after extended move = potential reversal. More responsive than golden/death cross (50/200) but more whipsaw-prone. Best combined with trend context.
SuperTrend ATR(10) × 3.0
Green line below price (uptrend) + Red line above price (downtrend)
ATR-based trend-following line that flips between support (below price, green) and resistance (above price, red). When price crosses the SuperTrend line, it reverses direction. Similar concept to Parabolic SAR but as a continuous line.
Look for: Color flip = trend change signal. Green line acting as dynamic support in uptrend. Red line acting as dynamic resistance in downtrend. Works best on higher timeframes. Wider multiplier (3.0) reduces whipsaws vs tighter settings.
ALMA ALMA(20, offset=0.85, sigma=6)
Cyan Gaussian-weighted moving average
Arnaud Legoux Moving Average uses a Gaussian distribution curve as weights. The offset parameter (0.85) shifts the bell curve toward recent prices, reducing lag. Sigma controls the width — lower = more responsive, higher = smoother.
Look for: ALMA slope change = early trend signal with minimal lag. Smoother than HMA with less overshoot. Good for trend direction and dynamic support/resistance. Compare with EMA — ALMA tracks price more closely during trends.
KAMA KAMA(10, fast=2, slow=30)
Yellow adaptive moving average
Kaufman Adaptive Moving Average adjusts its smoothing based on an Efficiency Ratio (direction / volatility). In trending markets it speeds up (approaches fast EMA), in choppy markets it slows down (approaches slow EMA). Developed by Perry Kaufman.
Look for: Flat KAMA = choppy, no trend. Sloped KAMA = trending. KAMA is the best MA for avoiding whipsaws in ranging markets while still catching trends. Price crossing KAMA is read as a trend-continuation condition, but only when KAMA itself is sloped.
Levels & Zones
CPR Monthly Central Pivot Range
Purple pivot/TC/BC + Green R levels + Red S levels
Pivot = (High + Low + Close) / 3 of previous month. TC (Top Central) and BC (Bottom Central) define the pivot range. R1-R3 resistance and S1-S3 support levels calculated from pivot. Server-side monthly calculation from daily data.
Look for: Price above pivot = bullish bias. Narrow CPR (TC ≈ BC) = trending day/week. Wide CPR = range-bound. R/S levels historically act as reaction and reversal zones.
CPR History 5 previous months
Past months' TC/Pivot/BC lines — shows where previous pivots cluster
Draws bounded TC, Pivot, and BC lines for the previous 5 months. Each month's levels only appear within that month's date range. Dimmer purple with dashed style.
Look for: Pivot clusters from multiple months = strong S/R zone. "Virgin" pivots (never tested) = levels price has not yet revisited.
Weekly CPR Weekly Central Pivot Range
Purple pivot/TC/BC + Green R levels + Red S levels
Pivot = (High + Low + Close) / 3 of previous week. R1-R3 resistance and S1-S3 support levels calculated from pivot. Faster-reacting than the monthly CPR, updating every Monday. Note: the main CPR indicator above also has its own timeframe setting (daily/weekly/monthly/quarterly/yearly) — this is a separate indicator, so you can enable it alongside a different CPR timeframe to show more than one pivot range on the chart at once.
Look for: Same read as monthly CPR, just on a weekly cadence. Narrow CPR (TC ≈ BC) = trending week, wide = range-bound.
Weekly CPR History previous weeks
Past weeks' TC/Pivot/BC lines — shows where previous weekly pivots cluster
Draws bounded TC, Pivot, and BC lines for previous weeks, each bounded to that week's date range.
Look for: Pivot clusters from multiple weeks = strong S/R zone, same read as monthly CPR History but at weekly resolution.
Daily CPR Daily Central Pivot Range
Purple pivot/TC/BC + Green R levels + Red S levels
Pivot = (High + Low + Close) / 3 of previous day. R1-R3 resistance and S1-S3 support levels calculated from pivot. Best used as an intraday reference point on lower timeframes.
Look for: Same read as monthly CPR, at daily resolution — most useful for intraday levels.
Daily CPR History previous days
Past days' TC/Pivot/BC lines — shows where previous daily pivots cluster
Draws bounded TC, Pivot, and BC lines for previous days, each bounded to that day's date range.
Look for: Pivot clusters from multiple days = intraday S/R zone.
Key Levels Previous D/W/M highs & lows
Previous daily, weekly, and monthly highs and lows drawn as horizontal lines
Draws the prior day's, prior week's, and prior month's high and low as flat lines extending across the chart, in a single color.
Look for: Previous highs/lows act as breakout and breakdown triggers. Price reacting on approach to one of these levels signals potential support/resistance.
Monday Range Mon H/L projected through the week
Monday's high and low extended as reference lines across the rest of the week
Captures the high and low set during Monday's session and projects them forward through the remaining trading days. An ICT (Inner Circle Trader) concept — Monday often sets the tone for that week's range.
Look for: A breakout beyond Monday's high or low is read as a signal for that week's directional bias. Price staying inside the range suggests consolidation.
Confluence Zones multi-source S/R clusters
Price areas where several signals stack up — drawn as shaded bands
Clusters CPR pivots, moving averages (incl. SMA/EMA 200, EMA 21), volume profile (POC, value area), swing highs/lows, Bollinger bands and key levels that sit close together. Band thickness/brightness scales with the weighted strength of the cluster; teal = support, red = resistance. Each band is labelled with its dominant source in plain English.
Look for: The thickest/brightest bands are the highest-confluence prices — where many independent signals agree. These tend to act as the strongest reaction levels. Information for your own analysis, not a recommendation.
Liquidation Heatmap modeled, from OI + price + long/short ratio
Yellow high modeled concentration + Purple low modeled concentration
A time x price heat layer showing where over-leveraged long and short positions may be sitting, inferred from our own Binance open-interest, price, and long/short-ratio history — never CoinGlass. These are modeled estimates: the leverage each position carries is assumed, not observed, so this is not the same as watching real liquidations happen on an order book.
Look for: Brighter/yellow bands mark higher modeled liquidation concentration and can act as price magnets during a squeeze. Dark areas carry little inferred exposure. Informational estimate for your own analysis, not observed liquidity and not advice.
S/R Channels pivot=10, maxChannels=6
Auto-detected support/resistance zones from pivot clustering
Scans 290 bars for pivot highs/lows (10-bar lookback each side). Groups nearby pivots into channels. Strength = number of pivot touches. Red resistance, Green support, gray when price is inside.
Look for: Multiple-touch channels = strongest levels. Channel flipping from support to resistance (or vice versa) = key breakout/breakdown confirmation.
Auto Fib lookback=265 bars
Automatic Fibonacci retracement levels from recent swing range
Finds highest high and lowest low in the last 265 bars. Draws Fibonacci levels at 0, 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%, 100%. Direction (up/down) determined by which extreme came first.
Look for: 61.8% is the "golden ratio" — strongest retracement. 38.2% is first significant pullback level. Price stalling at fib levels = potential reversal/continuation zone.
Fair Value Gap (FVG) ATR-filtered, mitigation tracking
Imbalance zones where price moved too fast — filtered by ATR threshold, with mitigation tracking
A 3-candle pattern where candle 1's wick doesn't overlap candle 3's wick, leaving an unfilled gap. Green = bullish FVG (below price), Red = bearish FVG (above). Auto-threshold filters out noise FVGs smaller than 25% of ATR(14). Mitigated FVGs shown dimmed (stop at fill point). Gaps shrink as price partially fills them.
Look for: Unfilled FVGs act as magnet zones — price tends to return to fill them. FVGs at key levels = high confluence. Multiple stacked FVGs = strong directional bias. Dimmed gaps = already tested, less significant.
Liq Zones drawing tool — AI-extracted from Coinglass heatmap
Drawing tool (not a chart indicator) — liquidation clusters extracted from Coinglass heatmap screenshots (approximate; accuracy depends on the image)
AI analyzes Coinglass liquidation heatmap screenshots to identify bright zones of concentrated liquidation levels. Rendered as drawings on the chart, not as a togglable indicator. Zones ranked by intensity (brightness). BTC/ETH only.
Look for: Bright liquidation clusters act as price magnets — large players may push price toward these zones to trigger cascading liquidations. Useful for identifying stop-hunt targets.
Volume & Flow
Volume with SMA(20) average
Bar volume with 20-period average line
Standard volume bars. Green bars = close ≥ open (up candle). Red bars = close < open (down candle). SMA(20) overlay shows average volume for context.
Look for: Volume spikes on breakouts = confirmation. Declining volume in trend = weakening. Volume climax (extreme spike) often marks reversals. Low volume at support = healthy base.
Volume Profile press V to toggle
Horizontal histogram showing volume distribution by price level
Aggregates volume at each price level across visible bars, weighted toward where each candle actually traded. Shows where most trading occurred. High-volume nodes (HVN) = value areas. Low-volume nodes (LVN) = fast-move zones. POC and value area prices are labeled on the price axis. Works on any asset, not just crypto. Toggle with V key. For a profile over a specific range, use the Volume Profile Range drawing tool — it is built from finer intrabar data, supports Total / Up-Down / Delta display (delta comes from real buyer-vs-seller trade data, not candle colors), and can be resized by dragging its edges.
Look for: Point of Control (POC) = highest volume price = magnet level. HVN = consolidation zones. LVN = price moves through quickly. Value Area (70% of volume) = expected range.
Naked POC crypto only
Dashed lines at past sessions’ points of control that price has never revisited
Each completed day gets a volume profile built from intrabar data; its POC is tracked forward. If price never trades back through that level, the POC stays “naked” and is drawn as a dashed line from its session to the right edge, labeled with its age. Touched levels disappear automatically. Up to 15 most recent naked levels over the last 90 days.
Look for: Naked POCs act as magnets — price often returns to fill them. Older untouched levels carry more weight. Confluence with other S/R strengthens the level.
OBV On Balance Volume
Green cumulative volume line
OBV adds volume on up-close bars and subtracts volume on down-close bars, creating a running total. The absolute value doesn't matter — the trend and divergences do. Developed by Joseph Granville.
Look for: Rising OBV + rising price = confirmed uptrend. Rising OBV + flat price = accumulation (bullish). Falling OBV + rising price = distribution (bearish divergence). OBV breakout before price breakout = leading signal.
CVD Spot from Binance kline taker_buy data
Cumulative Volume Delta — net buying vs selling pressure on spot
Calculated from Binance's taker buy base asset volume in each candle. CVD = cumulative sum of (taker_buy - taker_sell) volume. Shows whether buyers or sellers are more aggressive.
Look for: Rising CVD + rising price = healthy trend. Rising price + falling CVD = bearish divergence (distribution). CVD divergences are leading indicators for reversals.
CVD Spot Divergence same logic as RSI Divergence
Detects bullish/bearish divergences between price and CVD Spot
Finds swing highs/lows in price and compares them against Cumulative Volume Delta at the same points, using the same swing-detection logic as RSI Divergence.
Look for: Price making a new high/low while CVD fails to confirm = divergence, an early warning of trend exhaustion. Same read as RSI Divergence, fed by volume delta instead of momentum.
CVD Futures (USDT-M / COIN-M)
Cumulative Volume Delta from Binance perpetual futures markets
USDT-margined and COIN-margined futures taker buy/sell ratios. Cyan = USDT-M, Magenta = COIN-M. Separate panes below chart. Data from Binance derivatives APIs.
Look for: Futures CVD diverging from spot CVD = smart money positioning. COIN-M often reflects whale activity. Futures CVD spikes = leveraged conviction.
Open Interest from Binance futures
Amber rising / Purple falling — total open futures contracts
Historical open interest data from Binance USDT-M futures. Shows total number of outstanding futures contracts. Rendered as colored segments in a dedicated pane.
Look for: Rising OI + rising price = new longs (bullish). Rising OI + falling price = new shorts (bearish). Falling OI = positions closing. OI spike at extreme = potential squeeze.
Derivatives (Combined) CVD USDT-M + CVD COIN-M + OI
CVD Futures and Open Interest combined into a single pane
Plots USDT-margined CVD, COIN-margined CVD, and Open Interest together in one pane instead of three separate ones — a full derivatives read without eating up chart real estate.
Look for: Same signals as the individual CVD/OI indicators, read together for a faster derivatives-positioning check.
Structure
Market Structure BOS/CHoCH + Swing Labels
Break of Structure (BOS), Change of Character (CHoCH), swing labels (HH/HL/LH/LL), strong/weak highs and lows
Detects swing and internal market structure. BOS = trend continuation, CHoCH = reversal signal. Labels swing points as Higher High, Higher Low, Lower High, Lower Low. Marks strong/weak levels.
Look for: CHoCH = early reversal signal. BOS = trend continuation. HH+HL = bullish structure. LH+LL = bearish. Strong Low = protected level in uptrend.
Order Blocks Swing + Internal OBs
Institutional supply/demand zones from structure breaks + FVGs within SMC context
Identifies order blocks at BOS/CHoCH points — zones where institutional orders were placed. Swing OBs (colored) and internal OBs (gray). Includes SMC-context Fair Value Gaps.
Look for: Price returning to an unmitigated OB is historically a high-reaction zone. Bullish OB below price = support. Bearish OB above = resistance. Best on HTF for bias.
Equal H/L Equal Highs/Lows
Equal highs and equal lows — liquidity pools where stop orders accumulate
Detects swing points at similar price levels (within ATR threshold). These represent liquidity pools where stop-loss orders cluster.
Look for: Equal highs = sell-side liquidity (stops above). Equal lows = buy-side liquidity (stops below). Price often hunts these levels before reversing.
Value Zones Premium/Discount/Equilibrium
Premium, discount, and equilibrium zones based on swing range
Divides the current swing range into premium (above equilibrium), discount (below), and equilibrium (midpoint). Dotted line marks equilibrium.
Look for: Discount zones historically see higher demand; premium zones historically see higher supply. Equilibrium = fair value midpoint. Best used with structure context for confluence.
Market Sessions Asian / London / New York
Highlights the Asian, London, and New York trading sessions on the chart
Shades the chart to mark each of the three major trading sessions, so you can see at a glance which session was active over any bar.
Look for: Session opens/closes are key liquidity events. A range set during one session often gets swept during the next. Useful for intraday timing on lower timeframes.
Macro & Quant
M2 Money Supply right axis Premium
US M2 money supply overlaid on the price chart
Weekly-dated data published monthly by the Fed, plotted on a secondary (right) Y-axis. The classic macro-crypto leading indicator — M2 expansion has historically led BTC by roughly 10 weeks.
Look for: Rising M2 = more liquidity available for risk assets. Turning points in M2 growth matter more than the raw level.
Net Liquidity weekly, right axis Premium
Fed Balance Sheet minus TGA minus Reverse Repo
A direct measure of dollar liquidity in the banking system, plotted weekly on a secondary axis: Fed Balance − Treasury General Account − Reverse Repo.
Look for: Rising Net Liquidity is generally bullish for risk assets. Falling/draining liquidity is a headwind.
DXY (Dollar Index) daily, right axis Premium
US Dollar Index plotted alongside price
Daily DXY on a secondary Y-axis. Generally inverse to crypto — rising DXY is a headwind, falling DXY a tailwind.
Look for: Watch for divergences between DXY direction and price — when the usual inverse relationship breaks down, it is often notable.
Stablecoin Supply daily, right axis Premium
Total USDT supply overlaid on the price chart
Daily USDT total supply on a secondary axis. Rising supply reflects capital entering the crypto ecosystem (dry powder); declining supply reflects outflows.
Look for: A rapid increase in stablecoin supply is generally bullish; a sustained decline signals outflows.
Fear & Greed 0–100 Premium
Crypto Fear & Greed Index in its own pane
The widely-tracked Crypto Fear & Greed Index, scaled 0–100, plotted in a dedicated pane below the chart.
Look for: Extreme Fear (<25) often marks bottoms, Extreme Greed (>75) often marks tops. A classic contrarian read.
Funding Rate (3-exch) Binance/OKX/Bybit avg Premium
BTC perpetual funding rate averaged across three exchanges
BTC perpetual funding rate averaged across Binance, OKX, and Bybit, plotted in its own pane around a zero-line equilibrium.
Look for: Extreme positive funding = crowded longs. Extreme negative = capitulation/short-crowding. Zero-line crossovers mark equilibrium shifts.
BTC ETF Net Flow daily, $M Premium
Daily net flows into/out of US spot BTC ETFs
Daily net flows into or out of US spot BTC ETFs, in millions of dollars, plotted in a dedicated pane.
Look for: Positive flows = institutional buying. Negative = outflows. Sustained flows in either direction often signal a regime change.
VIX CBOE Volatility Index Premium
CBOE Volatility Index in its own pane
The CBOE Volatility Index, tracking equity-market implied volatility, plotted in a dedicated pane.
Look for: Above 30 = high fear, below 15 = complacency. VIX spikes tend to spill into risk-off moves across all assets, including crypto.
Yield Curve (10Y-2Y) Treasury spread Premium
10-year minus 2-year Treasury yield spread
The 10Y minus 2Y Treasury yield spread, plotted in its own pane.
Look for: Negative (inverted) is a classic recession signal. Steepening after an inversion often precedes macro bottoms and risk-on rallies.
Altcoin Season 0–100, 90-day window Premium
Percentage of top altcoins outperforming BTC over 90 days
The share of top altcoins beating BTC's return over a trailing 90-day window, scaled 0–100 in its own pane.
Look for: Above 75 = alt season. Below 25 = BTC dominance phase. Useful for timing portfolio rotation between BTC and alts.
Market Regime (global) HMM classification Premium
Background shading showing the prevailing market regime
An HMM (Hidden Markov Model) based regime classification, shown as background bands directly behind the price chart. Based on BTC returns, VIX, and the high-yield credit spread. Updates nightly.
Look for: Green bands = favorable/risk-on regime. Red bands = unfavorable/risk-off. Regime transitions are the moments worth paying attention to.
Risk Appetite PCA factor Premium
PCA-derived risk appetite factor in its own pane
A PCA-derived composite of VIX, the high-yield credit spread, TLT, and DXY, updated daily and plotted in a dedicated pane.
Look for: Positive = risk-on environment. Negative = risk-off. A macro risk-on/off gauge, not an asset-specific signal.
Liquidity Factor PCA factor Premium
PCA-derived liquidity factor in its own pane
A PCA-derived composite of M2, Net Liquidity, and the Fed balance sheet, updated daily and plotted in a dedicated pane.
Look for: Positive = expanding liquidity (bullish macro backdrop). Negative = tightening liquidity.
Credit Stress PCA factor Premium
PCA-derived credit stress factor in its own pane
A PCA-derived composite of the US high-yield spread, EU high-yield spread, and yield curve, updated daily and plotted in a dedicated pane.
Look for: Positive = credit stress rising (bearish, systemic-risk signal). Negative = calm credit conditions.
