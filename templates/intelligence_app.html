What is the Quant Engine?

The Quant Engine is Carrac's market intelligence system. It collects daily data from 11 sources across crypto, equities, macro, and sentiment — normalizes everything into comparable z-scores — then runs correlation, lead-lag, regime, factor, and chain analysis to surface patterns human eyes would miss.

Data collection started in March 2026. Price data (Binance, Yahoo Finance) is backfilled to 2017–2020 for most assets. FRED macro series go back to 2000 or later depending on the series. This gives the statistical models 6–25 years of daily observations to work with.

The engine currently tracks 45 canonical metrics across 6 domains (price, macro, derivatives, sentiment, on-chain, and composite factors), computes 820+ correlation pairs, monitors 38 lead-lag relationships at both daily and weekly resolution, and measures 3 macro transmission chains.

All analysis updates nightly. Eleven collectors run between 00:15 and 06:30 UTC. PCA factors, correlations, lead-lag (daily + weekly), regime detection, and anomaly clustering then run from 06:30 to 07:00 UTC. By 07:05 everything is fresh for the day.

How Z-Scores Work

Every metric is converted to a z-score: how many standard deviations the current value is from its rolling median. We use the Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) instead of standard deviation because it's resistant to outliers.

A z-score of +2.0 means the metric is 2 standard deviations above normal. A z-score of -1.5 means 1.5 below. Anything beyond ±2.0 is considered extreme.

Z-scores are computed over 90-day (primary, used for anomalies and conditions) and 30-day (short-term momentum) rolling windows. This is what makes it possible to compare VIX (which trades at 15-25) with BTC funding rates (which hover near 0.01%) on the same scale.

Methodology

Correlations (Tab 2) — Spearman rank correlations between 41 canonical metrics (~820 unique pairs). Recalculated daily over 60-day and 120-day rolling windows. Use the category filter pills to focus on specific domains (Price, Macro, Derivatives, Sentiment, Factors). Spearman is used instead of Pearson because it's robust to outliers and fat tails. Price series use log returns (not raw prices). Regime-dependent mode filters data by HMM state before computing correlations.

Lead-Lag (Tab 1, Overview) — Cross-correlations at different time lags for 38 focus pairs, including both direct macro→BTC pairs and intermediate chain links (e.g., FED_FUNDS→YIELD_2Y, YIELD_10Y→HY_SPREAD). Fast signals tested over ±14 days, slow signals over ±90 days. Uses a 730-day rolling window. Available at both daily and weekly frequency — weekly smooths noise and often produces stronger macro correlations. Persistence checking splits the window into two halves and verifies the lag is consistent.

Transmission Chains (Tab 8) — Three named macro propagation paths: Monetary Policy (FED_FUNDS → YIELD_2Y → YIELD_10Y → HY_SPREAD → SPX → BTC), Dollar Liquidity (M2 → NET_LIQUIDITY → DXY → GOLD → BTC), and Risk Appetite (VIX → HY_SPREAD → SPX → BTC). Each link shows its Spearman r, lag in days, and whether it's persistent. Chain strength is the geometric mean of |r| across all links. The weakest link shows where the macro signal degrades most.

PCA Composite Factors — Three factors built using Principal Component Analysis (numpy eigendecomposition, no sklearn). Risk Appetite combines VIX, HY_SPREAD, TLT, and DXY (44% explained variance). Liquidity combines M2, NET_LIQUIDITY, and FED_BALANCE (57% explained). Credit Stress combines HY_SPREAD, HY_SPREAD_EU, and YIELD_10Y2Y (69% explained). Each factor is the first principal component of its group, capturing the dominant shared signal. Sign convention ensures positive values match intuitive interpretation (e.g., high Risk Appetite = risk-on).

Regime Detection (Tab 3) — A 2-state Hidden Markov Model trained on BTC returns, VIX level, and HY credit spread. Learns favorable (higher returns, low VIX, tight spreads) and unfavorable (opposite) states. The transition matrix shows persistence probabilities. PELT change-point detection independently finds structural breaks in the data.

Conditional Analysis (Tab 4) — Filters historical dates where user-defined z-score conditions held simultaneously, then computes forward return distributions. The histogram shows what happened over the next N days (default 30). Hit rate, mean, median, and edge vs baseline are reported. The analogue search uses cosine similarity of 10-metric z-score vectors to find the most similar past environments.

Data Lab (Tab 7) — Aggregates z-scores across any combination of metrics, grouped by regime, z-score bucket, or time period. Uses CTE-based SQL with parameterized queries. Useful for answering questions like "what is the average VIX z-score during unfavorable regimes?" or "how does credit stress evolve quarter by quarter?"

Example Queries (Conditions Tab)

Risk-off: VIX:gt:2,HY_SPREAD:gt:1.5

Elevated fear + credit stress. If hit rate is 30%, BTC fell in 70% of such cases.

Euphoria: FEAR_GREED:gt:2,FUNDING_BTC:gt:1.5

Extreme greed + elevated funding (overleveraged longs). Often marks local tops.

Liquidity squeeze: M2:lt:-1,DXY:gt:1.5

Contracting money supply + strong dollar. Hostile for risk assets.

Interpreting results: <10 instances = anecdotal. 10-30 = hypothesis. 30+ = meaningful pattern if hit rate is consistently >60% or <40%. Large mean vs median gap = outlier skew.

Caveats

  • Not trading signals. This is a research tool. All analysis is backward-looking.
  • Multiple comparisons: With 27 metrics and many threshold combos, spurious patterns appear by chance. Treat results as hypotheses.
  • FRED publication lag: Macro data like M2 arrives weeks late. A "leading" correlation with M2 may partly reflect this lag.
  • HMM is in-sample: The regime model uses the full time series (including future data) to label past states. It's descriptive, not predictive.
  • Sparse data: COT (weekly), Google Trends (monthly), and some FRED series are forward-filled to daily. Correlations involving these have lower effective sample sizes.

Glossary & Abbreviations

TermMeaning
z-scoreHow many standard deviations a value is from its rolling median. +2 = very high, -2 = very low, 0 = normal.
r (correlation)Spearman rank correlation coefficient. Ranges from -1 (perfect inverse) to +1 (perfect positive). 0 = no relationship.
p-valueProbability of seeing this correlation by chance. Lower = more statistically significant. We show unadjusted values; <0.05 is conventionally "significant."
Hit ratePercentage of historical instances where the forward return was positive.
RegimeMarket state classified by the HMM: favorable (higher BTC returns, low VIX, tight spreads) or unfavorable (opposite).
HMMHidden Markov Model — a statistical model that infers hidden states (regimes) from observable data.
PELTPruned Exact Linear Time — an algorithm for detecting structural change points in time series data.
MADMedian Absolute Deviation — a robust measure of variability, used instead of standard deviation for z-score computation.
Cosine similarityMeasures how similar two vectors are by their angle (0-1). Used for historical analogue matching. 1.0 = identical direction.
Log returnln(price_today / price_yesterday). Used instead of % change because it's time-additive and symmetric.
Forward-fillCarrying the last known value forward until new data arrives. Used for weekly/monthly series to create daily data.
PCAPrincipal Component Analysis — extracts the dominant shared signal from a group of correlated metrics. PC1 (first principal component) captures the most variance.
Explained varianceHow much of the total variance PC1 captures. 69% for Credit Stress means the HY spreads + yield curve share a strong common factor.
Chain strengthGeometric mean of |r| across all links in a transmission chain. Measures overall signal quality from start (macro) to end (BTC).
PersistenceWhether a lead-lag relationship holds in both halves of the data window. Persistent = solid line in chain view. Non-persistent = dashed (may not be reliable).

Dashboard Info

Keyboard shortcuts: Press 1-9 to switch tabs. Click [?] buttons on section headers for detailed explanations of what each analysis shows and how to interpret it.

Charts: Powered by Apache ECharts. All charts support zoom (drag/scroll), pan, tooltips, and image export (camera icon top-right).

CSV export: Available on Overview (anomalies, z-scores), Correlations (full matrix), Regime (state history), and Data Lab (pivot results).

Caching: Heavy queries cached for 5 minutes via Redis. Data refreshes nightly by ~07:00 UTC.

Reliability: Collectors retry 3x with exponential backoff. Circuit breakers prevent hammering failing APIs. ntfy alerts on failures. Daily health checks at 07:30 UTC.
