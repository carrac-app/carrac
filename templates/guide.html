User Guide

Everything you can do in Carrac — from first login to advanced workflows.

Getting Started

What is Carrac?

Carrac is a multi-timeframe technical analysis platform for swing and position traders. It covers 337 curated assets (221 crypto, 116 traditional) across crypto, stocks, ETFs, indices, forex, and commodities — plus dynamic symbol search across multiple exchanges. Real-time crypto data from Binance, Hyperliquid, Bybit, MEXC, and Gate.io via WebSocket. US stocks and ETFs tick live during market hours via Finnhub. Daily data from Yahoo Finance for traditional markets.

The brand is written Carrac in long form, and the compact mark is [C⁴] — the four Cs of the Carrac name, to the fourth in brackets. You'll see [C⁴] as the browser tab favicon, the home-screen app icon, on sign-in and registration pages, and in the mobile header where the full wordmark won't fit.

ⓘ First time here? The chart area shows a short 3-step welcome guide (pick an asset, read the chart, connect your own AI) until you open your first asset.

Morning Routine (5–10 min)

  1. Glance at the sentiment bar (top of dashboard) for macro mood — Fear & Greed, MVRV-Z, Funding Rate
  2. Open the Morning Report (hamburger menu or Report view) — a single scrolling AI-generated brief
  3. Switch to Watchlist — scan signal badges (SQZ, V-CPR, TF-DIV, CC) for overnight changes
  4. Click any flagged asset → chart opens → quick timeframe scan (1D → 1W)
  5. Ask your own AI for a second opinion — with Carrac connected over MCP it reads the same chart you are looking at (see Integrations)

Finding Setups

  1. Sort by Consensio — find 7–9/9 (strong bull) or 0–2/9 (strong bear) extremes
  2. Check the EMA Ribbon column for SQZ/TGT badges (imminent flips)
  3. Open the chart → toggle indicators: CPR + EMA Ribbon + RSI Divergence
  4. Cycle through assets with , / . to confirm multi-timeframe alignment
  5. Create a play → use AI Validate for a structured scorecard
ⓘ Press Space anywhere to open the quick asset search. Type a symbol, pick from favourites, or build a ratio (e.g. GOLD/QQQ).

Dashboard Layout

Split Layout

The dashboard is divided into a chart on the left and a panel on the right, separated by a draggable divider. The chart displays the selected asset with indicators and drawing tools. The panel shows your asset list, signals, and contextual views.

View Modes

The right panel has a vertical strip of view mode buttons. Press E to cycle forward, Shift+E backward — cycling past either end collapses or expands the panel:

  • Lite — Minimal screener: symbol, price, change, consensio score
  • Consensio — Default view with Consensio, EMA Ribbon, signal badges, and score
  • Detail — Full screener with all columns: RSI, BB, CPR, ADX, Structure
  • News — Crypto, macro, and equities headlines (see News Panel)
  • Report — Morning report inline with afternoon delta
  • Plays — Active plays with condition progress tracking
  • Journal — Quick trade journal — log trades, see open positions and stats

The AI assistant isn't a view mode anymore — it floats above whichever view you're on. Open it anytime with / or the AI button.

Panel Tabs

Within each view mode, filter assets by tab:

  • Watchlist — Your personal symbols, organised into custom groups (star to add/remove, drag to reorder)
  • Crypto — All curated cryptocurrency assets
  • TradFi — Stocks, ETFs, indices, forex, commodities
  • All — Complete catalog across all categories

Watchlist Groups

Split your watchlist into named groups instead of one flat list. Click the ✎ (pencil) button in the controls row under the tabs to open the group editor — create, rename, or delete groups, and tick checkboxes to move symbols between them. Each group has its own “Add symbol…” box with suggestions; type any asset, even one outside the curated list, and press Enter to add it. Groups render as separate collapsible sections on the Watchlist tab. The star (★) still adds a symbol to your first group, and drag reorders within a group. Up to 50 groups of up to 100 symbols each — everything saves automatically and syncs to your account.

The same controls row also holds a filter box that narrows any tab to matching symbols or names as you type (press Esc to clear), a collapse-all toggle, and the ⚙ settings menu. The panel remembers your sort order and collapsed sections across reloads.

Bottom Drawer

Below the chart, a resizable drawer contains tabbed panels. Press Q to cycle tabs:

  • Positions — Log and track open futures/spot positions with live P&L
  • Alerts — Create and manage price/indicator/formula alerts
  • Scanner — Asset scores, labels, and sentiment data
  • Radar — Live zones, signals, transitions, positions, liquidations, and macro events for your watchlist
  • Backtest — Strategy backtester with entry/exit formulas

The drawer can be collapsed (click the chevron), expanded, or made fullscreen. Each tab has resizable left/right panes.

Chart Preview

Hover over any asset row in the panel to see a live preview chart tooltip. The preview is debounced and can be pinned by clicking.

News Panel

Browse crypto, macro, and equities headlines from inside Carrac. Press N or click the News button in the view-mode strip.

  • Add your own custom RSS sources from the gear menu — any valid feed URL works
  • Feed selection is independent from the header ticker bar, so you can have different sets of sources in each place

  • Click a card to expand inline — the summary and Read Full Article link slide down in place. Only one card stays open at a time

  • Header shows a live countdown (m:ss) to the next fetch
  • The panel also refreshes automatically when you return to the browser tab

Chart

Opening a Chart

Click any asset name in the panel to load it into the chart. The chart occupies the left side of the split layout. You can also:

  • Press Space, or click the search (magnifier) button in the toolbar next to the asset name, to search and jump to any symbol
  • Press , / . to cycle through your watchlist
  • Navigate to /chart/BTCUSDT/1D for a bookmarkable standalone URL

Chart Types

The toolbar shows the current chart style by name — click it (or right-click the chart background) to switch between:

  • Candles — Traditional OHLC candlestick chart (default)
  • Heikin Ashi — Smoothed candles that filter noise and show trend direction more clearly
  • Hollow Candles — Bullish candles are hollow (outline only), bearish are filled
  • OHLC Bars — Traditional bar chart with open/close ticks
  • Line / Area — Close price as a line with shaded area below

A labeled Panes button sits next to it for pane layouts — single, side-by-side, stacked, and rotation (see Multi-Chart).

Timeframes

Timeframe buttons appear in the toolbar. Two modes (toggle with the LTF/HTF switch):

  • HTF (Higher Timeframes): 4H, 1D, 3D, 1W, 1M — available to all users
  • LTF (Lower Timeframes): 1m, 3m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 1H — Member+

Keyboard shortcuts: 1=1D, 3=3D, 4=4H, 5=15m, H=1H, W=1W, M=1M

ⓘ TradFi assets (stocks, ETFs, etc.) support 1H, 4H, 1D, 3D, 1W, 1M. Sub-hourly timeframes are crypto-only due to data source limitations.

Navigation

  • Pan: Click and drag the chart area, or use ← → arrow keys
  • Zoom: Scroll wheel or + / − keys
  • Y-axis zoom: Drag the price axis to scale vertically
  • Reset view: Press G to snap back to the latest data
  • Center price: Press J to center the chart on the current price
  • Log scale: Press O or click the A/L buttons on the Y-axis

Crypto assets receive real-time price updates via WebSocket from multiple exchanges. Binance is primary, with automatic fallback to Hyperliquid, Bybit, MEXC, and Gate.io for coins not listed on Binance. US stocks and ETFs (~70 symbols including AAPL, NVDA, QQQ, SPY) tick live during market hours via Finnhub. TradFi daily data comes from Yahoo Finance.

Levels & Zones

Drawing Controls

Click a drawing to select it (it highlights on hover before you click). Overlapping drawings resolve predictably — handles beat lines beat edges beat bodies, and the top layer wins. Right-click a drawing to Bring to Front or Send to Back; clicking a stack repeatedly cycles through the overlapping drawings.

  • C — Cycle through drawing colours
  • + / − — Increase/decrease line thickness
  • [ / ] — Decrease/increase opacity
  • Del or Backspace — Delete selected drawing. Shift+Del for permanent delete
  • Ctrl+Z — Undo last drawing action
  • Ctrl+Y or Shift+Z — Redo

Positions in one click

The Long and Short position tools replace the old 3-click flow. One click on the chart places a full position box with a volatility-sized stop loss (1.5×ATR) and a 2:1 take-profit automatically. Drag the edges to resize the box; the right edge sets its width and that width is remembered for next time.

  • Alt+B — Cycle Long → Short → off
  • Long and Short tools are also in the drawing toolbar (press D to show it)

Nudge & multi-select

With a drawing selected, use the arrow keys to move it precisely. Hold Shift for a 10× jump.

  • ← / → — Move one candle left or right
  • ↑ / ↓ — Move one pixel up or down
  • Shift+arrow — Same, but ×10

To work on several drawings at once, Ctrl+click rectangles, channels, or position boxes to build a selection set. Drag any member to move all of them; arrow keys nudge the whole set; Del deletes all. Press Esc or click empty space to clear the selection. (Trendlines and Fibonacci tools are not yet multi-selectable.)

Clone with Ctrl+drag

Hold Ctrl and drag a rectangle or channel body — the original stays in place and a copy follows your cursor. Release to drop the clone. It lands on the undo stack like any other action.

Magnet

The toolbar magnet button cycles through three modes: off → weak → strong. In weak mode, a dragged or newly placed point snaps to the nearest candle O/H/L/C only when within about 8 pixels. In strong mode it always snaps. Hold Ctrl mid-drag to temporarily invert the current magnet setting.

Drawings panel & groups

Click the Drawings tab in the right drawer to see every drawing on the current chart in a list. Each row shows a readable label (positions display side, entry, TP/SL; shapes show colour and text). Click a row to jump the chart to that drawing and flash it.

Per-row controls: hide/show (eye icon), lock, delete. Drag rows to reorder the layer stack.

Use + Group to create a named group, then drag drawing rows onto a group header. Groups get their own hide/show toggle and you can rename them inline. Group membership and visibility sync across devices.

Copy, Paste & Duplicate

Reuse drawings without redrawing them:

  • Ctrl+C — Copy the selected drawing
  • Ctrl+V — Paste the copied drawing near the current crosshair
  • Right-click a drawing for Copy or Duplicate (duplicate places an offset copy immediately)
  • Right-click an empty chart area for Paste

Paste is same-asset only — you can't paste a BTC trendline onto ETH. Position and spot-buy markers are excluded from copy/paste on purpose (they track real trades). Every paste and duplicate is on the undo stack, so Ctrl+Z removes them cleanly.

Drawing Sync

Drawings are stored per-symbol on the server. When you draw on one device, it appears on all your other sessions within seconds (poll-based sync). Per-drawing hidden state (eye toggle) persists and syncs across devices too. Each drawing supports per-drawing CRUD — edit or delete individual drawings without affecting others.

ⓘ Drawings work in sub-panes too! You can draw trendlines on RSI, MACD, or any oscillator pane — not just the main price chart.

Custom Formulas

Build your own indicators with 80+ built-in TA functions — no coding required. Access via the indicator dropdown → "Custom" or the formula editor modal.

How It Works

  1. Open the formula editor (indicator menu → "+" or edit an existing custom indicator)
  2. Name your indicator and add one or more rows
  3. Each row is a formula expression like EMA(close, 21) - EMA(close, 55)
  4. Choose a display style (line, histogram, area, dots, etc.)
  5. Live preview updates as you type. Press Ctrl+Enter to save

Available Functions

SMA, EMA, WMA, Hull MA, RSI, MACD, Stoch RSI, OBV, ATR, Bollinger Bands, Keltner, Donchian, SuperTrend, Ichimoku components, Alligator, Z-Score, Percentile, Correlation, Beta, Consensio, and many more.

Structure & Stateful Functions

Functions for detecting market structure and tracking state across bars:

  • CUM(source) — Cumulative sum. Example: CUM(volume)
  • STREAK(condition) — Consecutive true count, resets to 0 on false. Example: STREAK(close > close[1])
  • HIGHEST_BARS(source, period) — Bars since highest in window (0 = this bar is the high). Example: HIGHEST_BARS(high, 50) == 0
  • LOWEST_BARS(source, period) — Bars since lowest in window
  • PIVOTHIGH(left, right) — Swing high detection, confirmed after right bars. Combine with VALUEWHEN(PIVOTHIGH(5,5) > 0, high) for last swing high level
  • PIVOTLOW(left, right) — Swing low detection

Variables

open, high, low, close, volume, hl2, hlc3, ohlc4 — plus any prior formula row by reference.

Operators

Arithmetic: + - * /. Comparison: > < >= <= == !=. Logic: AND OR NOT. Bar offset: [1] for previous bar, [n] for n bars ago.

Anchored CPR Functions

The formula engine has two families of CPR (Central Pivot Range) functions — they measure different things:

  • Rolling daily CPR (CPR_PIVOT(), CPR_R1()…CPR_S3()) — each bar's pivot is calculated from the previous bar's H/L/C. Useful for intraday momentum conditions.
  • Monthly anchored CPR (MONTHLY_CPR_P(), MONTHLY_CPR_TC(), MONTHLY_CPR_BC(), MONTHLY_CPR_R1()…MONTHLY_CPR_S3()) — anchored to the previous calendar month's high, low, and close. These return the same values as the chart's CPR indicator and step once per month. Use these when you want formula conditions that match what you see on the chart.

Example conditions:

  • MONTHLY_CPR_P() > CLOSE — price below monthly anchored pivot
  • CLOSE > MONTHLY_CPR_TC() AND VOLUME > SMA(VOLUME, 20) * 1.3 — price above monthly TC with volume confirmation
ⓘ For the full function reference, example recipes, and display style guide, see the Formula Engine Docs.

Custom formulas are available to every account.

Backtester

The backtester runs a formula against an asset’s own history and reports what would have happened. It lives in the chart’s bottom drawer under the Backtest tab, and it reads the same formula engine as Custom Formulas — anything you can write as an indicator condition, you can test here. Member+

Running one

  1. Open a chart, pick the timeframe you want to test on, and open the Backtest drawer tab.
  2. Write an Entry formula (when to open) and an Exit formula (when to close). Both are boolean expressions — e.g. CROSSOVER(EMA(9), EMA(21)) and CROSSUNDER(EMA(9), EMA(21)). Each field validates as you type.
  3. Set direction (Long, Short, or Both — reverse on each signal), starting capital, position size, commission, and an optional date range.
  4. Click Run Backtest. Trades are marked on the chart itself, so you can see where each one opened and closed.

What the results tell you

Results report win rate, profit factor and total return per trade set, plus portfolio-level max drawdown, Sharpe, Sortino and Calmar. The Sharpe figure is scaled to the timeframe you tested on, so a 4-hour test and a daily test are not silently on different scales. Every trade is listed individually, and CSV exports the full trade list for your own analysis.

Regime filter

The Regime selector splits results by the market regime that was in force at the time — All, Favorable, or Unfavorable. This answers a question a plain backtest cannot: whether an edge is real or just an artefact of one long trending stretch. When you filter to a subset, the portfolio-level metrics (Sharpe, drawdown) are greyed out, because they are only meaningful across the whole run.

Realism & risk controls

Collapsed by default under Realism & Risk Controls, because the defaults are deliberately plain and you should turn these on knowingly:

  • Slippage — price penalty applied on both entry and exit, in basis points.
  • Stop Loss % / Take Profit % — percentage from entry. 0 disables them.
  • Stop Type — Close only checks exits on the bar close; Intrabar checks the bar’s high and low. A stop or target set while Stop Type is on Close only has no effect — if you set one, switch to Intrabar.
  • Entry Fill — Next-bar open (the default) fills on the bar after the signal, because in live trading you cannot act on a close before it has happened. Signal-bar close fills on the signal bar itself — that is look-ahead bias and will flatter the result. Use it for studying a signal, not for judging a strategy.
  • Instrument — Spot or Perpetual. Perpetual applies funding costs over the holding period.
ⓘ When a stop and a target would both be hit on the same bar, the stop is applied first — the engine does not try to guess which came first inside the bar. Results are therefore the pessimistic reading of an ambiguous bar, by design.

What the backtester assumes

Three of these settings are assumptions rather than measurements — the bar data genuinely cannot settle them, so the engine has to choose. Every one of them moves the result, and the defaults are the conservative reading in each case:

AssumptionDefaultThe other reading
When a bar hits both the stop and the target, which one countsThe stopTaking the target instead flatters the result

What happens when the market gaps straight past a stopYou get the gap priceAssuming the stop price itself pretends you were filled where nobody traded

These barely matter on assets that trade around the clock, because one bar’s open is essentially the last one’s close. On anything with a weekend or an overnight session they matter a great deal.

Over MCP all three are settable, and every result comes back with an assumptions block naming which were used and which way each bends the numbers — so an assistant reporting a backtest can tell you whether it was measured honestly or flattered.

The Consensio Method

The Consensio Method is a multi-timeframe position management system developed by the late D. Tyler Jenks (1950–2019), founder of Lucid Investment Strategies LLC. Originally designed for stock portfolio management on the weekly timeframe, it is also widely applied to crypto, especially to Bitcoin’s higher timeframes. Carrac implements the method; it did not invent it. This section describes what the method holds — none of it is a recommendation from Carrac.

The Core Idea

Jenks built the method around a portfolio question: how much exposure does the current trend support?

The method answers it by reading the alignment of three moving averages across three timeframes. In Jenks’s framing, full bullish alignment corresponds to a full position, a breakdown in alignment to scaling back, and full bearish alignment to no position at all. The idea is that a trend-following rule keeps a holder in strong trends while exposure falls as momentum fades.

The Three Moving Averages

On each timeframe, Consensio plots three Simple Moving Averages:

  • Short MA (3-period) — fast, reactive to recent price action
  • Medium MA (7-period) — intermediate momentum filter
  • Long MA (30-period) — underlying trend anchor

The ordering of these three lines determines the signal strength on that timeframe:

MA OrderSignalPositionsMeaning
Short > Medium > Long+33 / 3Strongest bullish — all MAs stacked upward
Medium > Short > Long+22 / 3Bullish but short MA pulling back
Short > Long > Medium+11 / 3Early bullish or weakening trend
Medium > Long > Short−10 / 3Early bearish — short crossed below long
Long > Short > Medium−20 / 3Bearish — long MA dominant
Long > Medium > Short−30 / 3Strongest bearish — all MAs stacked downward

Three Timeframes, Nine Packages

Carrac calculates Consensio on three timeframes simultaneously:

  • 1-Day (1D) — short-term trend direction
  • 3-Day (3D) — intermediate confirmation
  • 1-Week (1W) — the dominant trend anchor

Each timeframe contributes 0 to 3 positions, for a combined score of 0 to 9. The method frames the score as nine equal “packages” of a position, each one added or removed as the moving averages align or break apart across the three timeframes.

Reading the Score

  • 9/9 — Full consensus: every timeframe stacked bullish.
  • 6–8/9 — Strong alignment with a pullback on one timeframe.
  • 3–5/9 — Mixed: some timeframes bullish, others not.
  • 1–2/9 — Weak or emerging bullish alignment.
  • 0/9 — No bullish alignment on any timeframe.

In Jenks’s method the score maps directly to position size — all nine packages at 9/9, none at 0/9. Carrac shows the score; it does not size anything for you.

In the screener, the score is colour-coded: green for 6–9, orange for 3–5, grey for 1–2, and red for 0. On the chart, enabling the Consensio indicator (I → Consensio) draws the three SMA lines and shows a summary table in the top-right corner with per-timeframe positions and the total.

What the Method Holds

Stay with strong trends. The method holds that most of a trend-follower’s results come from staying in strong trends, and that scaling exposure with alignment is a disciplined way to do it: adding as momentum confirms across timeframes, reducing as alignment fades.

Multi-timeframe confirmation reduces noise. A single timeframe produces many short-lived crosses. Requiring agreement across the daily, 3-day and weekly charts filters most of them out, so the score moves on broader trend shifts rather than on every wiggle.

A smoother ride is the stated trade-off. Proponents of the method argue that scaling in gradually gives up part of a parabolic top, while scaling out as alignment fades avoids part of the deep drawdowns. Carrac publishes no backtest of that claim; the Backtest tab lets you test a Consensio rule against an asset’s own history.

It is a bird’s-eye view, not a crystal ball. Consensio gives a high-level overview of where an asset stands. There is no underlying magic — the score depends entirely on price action and moving-average alignment. It reads what the market is doing right now across multiple timeframes, nothing more. That transparency is a feature: you always know exactly what drives the score and can verify it yourself on the chart.

Reading It Carefully

ℹ Jenks presented Consensio as a framework, not a rigid set of commands: in the method the score is a ceiling on exposure, not an instruction to fill every package. In Carrac it is a reading of moving-average alignment, nothing more.
  • 0/9 does not mean “bottom.” It says the trend is bearish on all timeframes. Consensio is a trend-following method, not a bottom-picking one, so a 0/9 reading says nothing about where a low will form.
  • 1/9 is the faintest reading. One timeframe with a weak bullish order while two are bearish is the least alignment the score can register.
  • 9/9 marks full consensus, which is rarely permanent. From 9/9 the score can only hold or fall, which is why the method’s scaling-down rules (below) start to matter there.
  • Timeframe divergence. When the daily turns bullish while the weekly stays bearish (or the reverse), the timeframes disagree. The method treats such divergences as a possible early sign of a trend change.

Choppy Markets — The Limitation

Consensio is a trend-following method, and trend-following struggles in sideways, range-bound markets. When price chops back and forth without a clear direction, the moving averages keep crossing each other, and a rule that follows them adds and removes packages over and over — the classic whipsaw cost of trend-following.

If the score flickers between, say, 3/9 and 5/9 without ever committing to a strong trend, the market is likely in a range. The score is then less informative moment to moment, and the range high and range low (markable with the drawing tools or the key levels indicator in Carrac) describe the market better, until a decisive breakout (a sustained move to 7+/9 or a drop to 1–/9) ends the range and the trend reading becomes meaningful again.

Scaling Down in the Method

When the score has been at 9/9 and starts declining — to 8, then 7, then lower — that decline is what the method’s scaling design is built around: exposure is meant to come back down as alignment fades, the same way it went up as alignment built. In Jenks’s framing that is the system working as designed, not a failure: for packages added on the way up at lower prices, scaling down is how unrealized gains become realized ones.

The method’s argument is behavioural: reducing a position as gains shrink feels like giving up, so a rule set in advance replaces a decision made under pressure.

Reduction Without Selling

Practitioners of the method, especially long-term Bitcoin holders, often describe a reduction in the score as a reduction in net exposure, not necessarily a sale of the spot position. The instruments usually named for that:

  • Put options — A contract that gains value as price drops, offsetting losses on a spot position. Functions as insurance for a holding.
  • Futures shorts — A short futures position that offsets long spot exposure, partially or fully.
  • Perpetual swaps — Similar to futures but without expiration, commonly used on crypto exchanges.
  • Options collars — A combined short-call, long-put position that creates a cost-neutral hedge band.

In that reading, a fall from, say, 7/9 to 4/9 can be met by hedging the difference while the coins stay put, and the hedge is closed when alignment recovers. Whether to do any of this, and how, is the holder’s own decision.

  1. Open any chart, add the Bollinger Bands indicator if not already on.

Setup B — BB Fade (Engulfing) Play

Use case: "Alert me when BTC reached the upper band AND a bearish engulfing prints on the same bar — that's my short signal." Symmetric for long-fade (below lower + bullish engulfing).

  1. Plays → New Play. Primary asset, direction (long / short), timeframe.
  2. + Add condition → Candle → BB Fade (Engulfing).
  3. Params:
    • direction: bearish (fade short — price extended up, reverse down) or bullish (fade long — price extended down, reverse up)

  4. Logic: All-of. Save.

Fires when both bb_position reaches the matching edge AND the matching-direction engulfing prints on the same bar close.

Setup C — BB Breakout-Retest (Engulfing) Play

Use case: "Alert me when BTC broke above the upper band a few bars ago, has now pulled back to within 0.5% of the middle band, and a bullish engulfing prints. That's my long entry on the trend continuation." Classic throwback / first-pullback trade.

  1. Plays → New Play. Primary asset, direction, timeframe.
  2. + Add condition → Candle → BB Breakout-Retest (Engulfing).
  3. Params:
    • direction: bullish (upper-band breakout → pullback down to middle → bullish engulfing) or bearish (lower-band breakout → bounce up to middle → bearish engulfing)
    • timeframe: matches the Play (1H / 4H / Daily / 3-Day / Weekly)
    • lookback_bars: how many bars back to look for the prior breakout (default 15; range 5–50)
    • proximity_pct: how close to the middle band counts as "at retest" (default 0.5%; range 0.1–2.0)
    • require_volume: ticked (uses volume-confirmed engulfings only)
  4. Logic: All-of. Save.

Fires when all three conditions align on a bar close: (1) a close past the band within the lookback window, (2) current close within the proximity of the middle band, (3) matching-direction engulfing on the current bar.

Setup D — Composable Play (custom BB parameters)

The dedicated rules (Setups B and C) use default Bollinger parameters — period 20, std-dev 2.0, offset 0 — for keep-it-simple UX. If you want a BB strategy with non-default parameters, compose it manually:

  1. Plays → New Play → + Add condition → Indicator State → BB Position. Pick state (inside / above upper band / below lower band). This reads the BB state from precompute (uses default BB params).
  2. + Add condition → Engulfing detected. Pick direction and timeframe.
  3. Logic: All-of. Save.

Functionally identical to Setup B (fade) when paired correctly. For a continuation pattern with a "within last N bars" lookback and a custom BB offset, use the dedicated Setup F — BB Breakdown Continuation rule (below) instead of composing by hand.

Setup E — BB Squeeze Breakout (Engulfing) Play

Use case: "Alert me when BTC's Bollinger Bands compressed (squeezed) for several consecutive bars in the last 10, the squeeze has now released, and the current bar broke above the upper band on above-average volume — that's my long entry on the volatility expansion." Classic TTM Squeeze pattern; the symmetric short fires on a close below the lower band.

  1. Plays → New Play. Primary asset, direction, timeframe.
  2. + Add condition → Candle → BB Squeeze Breakout.
  3. Params:
    • direction: bullish (close above upper band after squeeze) or bearish (close below lower band after squeeze)
    • timeframe: matches the Play (1H / 4H / Daily / 3-Day / Weekly)
    • lookback_bars: how recently the squeeze must have fired (default 10; range 5–30)
    • min_squeeze_bars: minimum consecutive bars of squeeze to qualify (default 3; range 1–20). Higher values filter out weak / transient compressions.
    • require_volume: ticked — the breakout close must have volume above its 20-bar SMA
  4. Logic: All-of. Save.

Fires on bar close when all four conditions align: (1) a squeeze run of at least min_squeeze_bars happened within the lookback window, (2) the current bar is no longer in squeeze, (3) the current close breaks past the matching band, (4) volume confirmation if enabled.

Setup F — BB Breakdown Continuation Play

Use case: "Short BTC when price closed below the lower band within the last several bars, then either a bearish engulfing prints or price retests back up to the lower band — using my +10 BB offset." The continuation cousin of Setup C, with three differences: the retest can target the broken band (not just the middle), the two confirmations are combined with OR, and — uniquely among the dedicated Play rules — it accepts custom BB parameters including offset. The symmetric long fires on an upper-band breakout.

  1. Plays → New Play. Primary asset, direction, timeframe.
  2. + Add condition → Candle → BB Breakdown Continuation.
  3. Params:
    • direction: bearish (breakdown below lower band → short) or bullish (breakout above upper band → long)
    • timeframe: matches the Play (1H / 4H / Daily / 3-Day / Weekly)
    • lookback_bars: how many bars back the breakout may have happened (default 15; range 5–50)
    • confirm_mode: either (engulfing OR retest), engulfing only, or band_retest only
    • retest_band: broken (the band that was broken — lower for short, upper for long) or middle (Setup-C-style mid-band pullback)
    • proximity_pct: how close to the retest band counts (default 0.5%; range 0.1–2.0; ignored when confirm_mode is engulfing-only)
    • BB offset: shift the bands ±N bars (default 0; range −20 to 20). Positive lags the bands TradingView-style — e.g. +10 compares price to where the band was 10 bars ago.
  4. Logic: All-of. Save.

Fires on bar close when (1) a close past the band happened within the lookback window, and (2) the chosen confirmation is met — a matching-direction engulfing on the current bar, a retest of the chosen band, or either. Quant Engine

Most technical analysis looks at a single asset in isolation. It tells you whether the wind is at your back or in your face.

  • Conditional Backtesting — "What happened to BTC when the Fear & Greed was below 20 and the yield curve was inverted?" Query historical return distributions under specific macro conditions

Z-Scores & Anomaly Detection

Every metric gets a 30-day and 90-day rolling z-score computed nightly. A z-score tells you how many standard deviations the current value is from its recent average.

  • |z| < 1 — Normal range
  • |z| 1–2 — Noteworthy. Worth monitoring
  • |z| ≥ 2 — Extreme. Flagged as anomaly. Historically rare (<5% of days)

When 3 or more extreme metrics from 2+ different domains occur simultaneously, the engine flags an anomaly cluster — a potential structural shift in the macro environment.

Regime Detection

A 2-state Hidden Markov Model (HMM) classifies each day as either Favourable or Unfavourable based on three inputs: BTC returns, VIX level, and high-yield credit spread.

  • State probability — How confident the model is (e.g. 94% favourable)
  • Duration — How many days the current regime has lasted
  • Transition matrix — Historical probability of switching between states
  • Changepoints — Structural breaks detected via PELT algorithm, marking when the regime actually shifted

Regime state is integrated into the morning report, scanner context, and conditional backtest queries.

Correlations

Spearman rank correlations computed across all 82 metrics in 60-day and 120-day rolling windows. Spearman (rather than Pearson) because financial returns have fat tails and outliers.

Use this to answer questions like:

  • "Is BTC currently correlated with SPX or decoupling?"
  • "Is the DXY-crypto inverse relationship holding?"
  • "Which macro metric has the strongest relationship with ETH right now?"

The UI provides a heatmap (filterable by domain: Price, Macro, Derivatives, Sentiment, Factors), a network graph, and a detail table with exact r-values and p-values.

Regime toggle

Next to the 60d/120d window selector there is an All / Favorable / Unfavorable toggle. Switching to Favorable or Unfavorable splits the sample by the HMM regime state for each day, so you can see how correlations change in different market environments — for example whether BTC’s relationship with SPX is tighter in risk-off periods than in calm ones.

Non-significant pairs are muted

Cells where the correlation does not pass a false-discovery-rate test (FDR q ≥ 0.05) are rendered in a muted / washed-out style rather than a saturated colour. This prevents the heatmap from looking busy with noise. Hover a cell to see the exact r-value, p-value, and q-value in the tooltip. A legend beneath the heatmap explains the muting. The detail table has a q-value column so you can sort or filter by statistical reliability.

Lead-Lag Analysis

Some metrics consistently move before others. The engine computes cross-correlograms for 52 defined pairs to find the optimal lead time.

⚠ How to read lead-lag signals: The engine scans many candidate lags and picks the strongest one for each pair. Treat any finding as an exploratory hypothesis, not a reliable forecast. Prefer signals marked stable — those held up across multiple rolling sub-windows. Signals marked ⚠ are less consistent and should be weighted accordingly. The assoc. band shown next to a projected move is the 95 % confidence interval of the correlation coefficient scaled by the leader’s current z-score — it quantifies uncertainty in the association, not a predictive interval for future prices.

Signal persistence badges

Each lead-lag signal now shows one of two badges:

  • stable — The lead-lag relationship held across multiple rolling sub-windows. Higher confidence that the pattern is structural rather than an artefact of the sample period.
  • ⚠ — The relationship is less consistent across sub-windows. Treat it as a weak or tentative signal — worth noting, but not as a strong prior.

Transmission Chains

Risk Appetite Chain
VIX → HY_SPREAD → SPX → BTC
Volatility spikes hit credit first, then equities, then crypto. Fastest chain — lags of days to weeks.

Composite Factors (PCA)

Three higher-level factors derived via Principal Component Analysis that distil multiple related metrics into a single signal:

  • RISK_APPETITE = PCA(VIX, HY_SPREAD, TLT, DXY) — Broad risk conditions. Higher = more risk-averse
  • LIQUIDITY = PCA(M2, NET_LIQUIDITY, FED_BALANCE) — Monetary/fiscal liquidity. Higher = more accommodative
  • CREDIT_STRESS = PCA(HY_SPREAD, HY_SPREAD_EU, YIELD_10Y2Y) — Credit market stress. Higher = more stress

These factors are tracked as canonical metrics — they flow through correlations, lead-lag, and z-scores like any other metric.

Conditional Backtest

The most powerful query tool. Define z-score conditions (e.g. VIX:gt:2 + FEAR_GREED:lt:-1.5) and see what happened historically when all conditions held simultaneously.

How to Use It

  1. Go to Quant Engine → Conditions tab
  2. Add conditions using the builder (or pick a preset like "Risk-Off Extreme" or "Capitulation Setup")
  3. Set the target asset (default: BTC) and forward horizons (7, 30, 90 days)
  4. View the results: number of historical instances, forward return distribution (mean, median, hit rate), and whether today matches

Example: "What happened to BTC over the next 30 days when Fear & Greed z-score was below −1.5 AND the yield curve (10Y-2Y) was inverted?"
Result: 18 instances, median return +12.4%, hit rate 72% — historically associated with positive forward returns.

Historical Analogues

Given today's macro z-score vector, find the most similar historical dates using cosine similarity across 9 core metrics (VIX, HY_SPREAD, YIELD_10Y, YIELD_10Y2Y, M2, NET_LIQUIDITY, FED_FUNDS, FEAR_GREED, COT positioning, FUNDING). Then look at what happened next.

Useful for: "The current macro setup looks like early 2023 — what did BTC do from there?"

Data Lab

Pivot-table queries for custom exploration. Group metrics by regime state or calendar month, aggregate by mean or median. Useful for spotting seasonal patterns or regime-dependent behaviour.

What You Can Build With This

Morning Briefing in Your Terminal
Ask your AI CLI: "Get the morning report, check my active plays, and tell me if anything needs attention today." The AI calls report_get_compact, play_get_digest, and alert_list_triggered and gives you a synthesised verbal briefing.
Conversational Trade Planning
"I think ETH is setting up for a breakout above the 200 EMA. Create a play with RSI above 50 and DXY below 103 as conditions." The AI calls play_create with structured conditions, and later play_evaluate to track them.
Automated Journal From CLI
"I just bought 0.5 BTC at 68,400 with a stop at 66,500." The AI calls trade_log. When you close: "Close my BTC trade at 71,200, I took profit manually." The AI updates the journal and can run trade_get_ai_review for feedback.
Macro Screening Agent
Build a recurring script that calls quant_get_data for z-scores, scanner_get_screen for verdicts, and market_get_calendar for events — then has an AI summarise the macro picture. Push the result to Telegram or ntfy.
Cross-Asset Research
"What happened historically when the yield curve inverted and crypto funding was negative?" The AI calls backtest_get_historical_analog with your specific z-score conditions and reports the forward return distributions.
Portfolio Dashboard in Obsidian / Notion
Use the REST API to pull /api/plays/active, /api/alerts, and /api/journal/trades into any note-taking tool. Build a personal dashboard that stays in sync with your Carrac data.
ⓘ The MCP server uses the same data as the web UI — same scanner scores, same indicators, same plays. What you see in the app is exactly what your AI assistant gets.
